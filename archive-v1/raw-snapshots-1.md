## BITwiki Epistemic Tiers/Overview

Source: https://bitwiki.org/BITwiki_Epistemic_Tiers/Overview

```mediawiki
This document defines confidence levels for BITwiki content. These are markers of our current assessment, not fixed truths. The epistemic tier system provides a structured framework for communicating the evidentiary status of knowledge claims within the BITwiki ecosystem, enabling readers to make informed judgments about the reliability and maturity of presented information.
The framework draws upon established epistemological traditions, including the philosophy of science (Popper, 1959; Kuhn, 1962), social epistemology (Goldman, 1999), and contemporary knowledge management theory (Nonaka & Takeuchi, 1995). By explicitly marking confidence levels, we acknowledge the provisional nature of empirical knowledge while maintaining rigorous standards for claim validation. --- [[Category:BITwiki Epistemic Tiers]]
```

Source: https://bitwiki.org/Bioluminescent_Organisms/Applications

```mediawiki
The applications of bioluminescence extend far beyond natural phenomena, with significant potential in scientific research, medicine, environmental monitoring, and biotechnology. The [[Fluorescent Coral Reefs]] implementation represents one such application focused on environmental monitoring.

Research applications include:
* [[Reporter Genes]] in molecular biology (using GFP and related proteins)
```

## Bioluminescent Organisms/Biochemical Mechanism

Source: https://bitwiki.org/Bioluminescent_Organisms/Biochemical_Mechanism

```mediawiki
The biochemical mechanism of bioluminescence involves a complex series of reactions centered around the interaction of [[Luciferin]] and [[Luciferase]]. While the basic principle is consistent across bioluminescent organisms, the specific molecules and mechanisms vary significantly between different species.

The core reaction can be summarized as:
<latex>\text{Luciferin} + \text{O}_2 + \text{ATP} \xrightarrow{\text{Luciferase}} \text{Oxyluciferin} + \text{Light} + \text{AMP} + \text{PP}_i</latex>

Different organisms use different variants of [[Luciferin]] and [[Luciferase]], leading to variations in the color and intensity of emitted light. The reaction is regulated by [[Enzyme Kinetics]] principles, with factors such as pH, temperature, and cofactor concentrations affecting the efficiency of light production.
```

## Bioluminescent Organisms/Examples

Source: https://bitwiki.org/Bioluminescent_Organisms/Examples

```mediawiki
Bioluminescence occurs in various organisms across multiple taxonomic groups, each with unique mechanisms and ecological functions. These examples demonstrate the diversity and adaptability of bioluminescent systems in nature.

Marine organisms represent the majority of bioluminescent species:
* [[Dinoflagellates]] (marine plankton) - responsible for glowing waves
* [[Jellyfish]] (Aequorea victoria) - source of green fluorescent protein (GFP)
* [[Fireflies]] (Photinus pyralis) - terrestrial insects with controlled light production

Each organism has evolved distinct bioluminescent systems adapted to their specific ecological niches and survival strategies.
```

## Bioluminescent Organisms/Overview

Source: https://bitwiki.org/Bioluminescent_Organisms/Overview

```mediawiki
Bioluminescence is the production and emission of light by living organisms, a form of [[Chemiluminescence]] where light energy is released by a chemical reaction. This phenomenon is found in various organisms across different taxonomic groups, including bacteria, fungi, marine invertebrates, and fish.

The underlying principle of bioluminescence involves the enzyme [[Luciferase]] acting on the pigment [[Luciferin]] in the presence of [[Adenosine Triphosphate|ATP]] and [[Oxygen]], producing light with minimal heat generation. This reaction is highly efficient, converting nearly 100% of the chemical energy into light, making it one of the most efficient light-producing reactions known.
```

```mediawiki
Several theoretical frameworks and related concepts support the understanding of bioluminescent organisms and their applications. These theories provide the scientific foundation for both natural bioluminescence and engineered implementations like the [[Fluorescent Coral Reefs]] project.

These theoretical frameworks help guide the development of synthetic bioluminescent systems and inform the design of bioengineered applications.
```
